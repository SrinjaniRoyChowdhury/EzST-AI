# GST Knowledge Base – Core Rules & Compliance

## What is GST?
Goods and Services Tax (GST) is a comprehensive indirect tax levied on the supply of goods and services in India. It replaced multiple indirect taxes and came into effect on 1 July 2017 under the Constitution (One Hundred and First Amendment) Act, 2016.

## GST Components
- **CGST** (Central GST): Collected by Central Government on intra-state supplies.
- **SGST** (State GST): Collected by State Government on intra-state supplies.
- **IGST** (Integrated GST): Collected by Central Government on inter-state supplies and imports.
- **UTGST**: Applicable in Union Territories instead of SGST.

## GST Rates
Standard GST rates: 0%, 5%, 12%, 18%, 28%.
- 0%: Essential commodities (food grains, milk, eggs, etc.)
- 5%: Mass consumption items (edible oil, sugar, spices, tea)
- 12%: Processed food, computers, mobile phones
- 18%: Most services, electronics, capital goods
- 28%: Luxury items, automobiles, tobacco, aerated drinks

## GSTIN Format
A GSTIN (GST Identification Number) is a 15-character alphanumeric code:
- Characters 1-2: State code (e.g., 27 for Maharashtra, 07 for Delhi)
- Characters 3-12: PAN number of the taxpayer
- Character 13: Entity number (1-9, A-Z)
- Character 14: 'Z' by default
- Character 15: Check digit

Example: 27AABCU9603R1ZM

## GST Registration
Mandatory registration for businesses with annual turnover exceeding:
- ₹40 lakhs (goods) or ₹20 lakhs (services) in most states
- ₹20 lakhs (goods) or ₹10 lakhs (services) in special category states

## Tax Invoice Requirements
A valid GST tax invoice must contain:
1. Name, address, and GSTIN of supplier
2. Consecutive serial number (up to 16 characters)
3. Date of issue
4. Name, address, and GSTIN of recipient (for B2B)
5. HSN/SAC code for goods/services
6. Description of goods/services
7. Quantity and unit
8. Total value of supply
9. Taxable value and discounts
10. Rate and amount of CGST, SGST, IGST, CESS
11. Place of supply (state name and code)
12. Signature or digital signature

## HSN Codes
Harmonised System of Nomenclature (HSN) codes classify goods:
- Turnover > ₹5 crore: 6-digit HSN code mandatory
- Turnover ₹1.5–5 crore: 4-digit HSN code
- Turnover < ₹1.5 crore: HSN code optional (but recommended)

## SAC Codes
Services Accounting Codes (SAC) classify services. All SAC codes are 6 digits beginning with 99.

## GST Returns

### GSTR-1
- Filed by: Registered suppliers (outward supplies)
- Due date: 11th of next month (monthly) / 13th of next quarter (quarterly)
- Contains: Details of all outward supplies (B2B, B2C, exports, debit/credit notes)

### GSTR-2A
- Auto-populated from sellers' GSTR-1 filings
- Buyers use this to verify ITC (Input Tax Credit) claims
- Read-only; cannot be modified by buyer

### GSTR-3B
- Summary return filed monthly by all taxpayers
- Contains: Net tax liability after ITC set-off
- Due date: 20th of next month for most taxpayers

### GSTR-9
- Annual return filed once a year
- Due date: 31 December of the following financial year

## Input Tax Credit (ITC)
ITC allows businesses to reduce their tax liability by claiming credit for taxes paid on purchases.

### ITC Eligibility Conditions:
1. You must possess a valid tax invoice or debit note
2. The supplier must have filed GSTR-1 and the invoice appears in GSTR-2A
3. Goods/services must have been received
4. Tax must have been paid to the government by the supplier
5. GST return must have been filed

### ITC Blocked Credits (Section 17(5)):
- Motor vehicles (with exceptions)
- Food and beverages
- Outdoor catering
- Club memberships
- Health and life insurance (with exceptions)
- Works contract services for immovable property

## E-Way Bill
Required for movement of goods worth more than ₹50,000.
- Generated on the e-Way Bill portal
- Valid for 1 day per 200 km (or part thereof)
- Mandatory for inter-state movement regardless of value

## Credit Notes and Debit Notes

### Credit Note (Section 34):
Issued by supplier when:
- Taxable value or tax charged is more than what is payable
- Goods are returned by the recipient
- Services are found to be deficient

### Debit Note (Section 34):
Issued by supplier when:
- Taxable value or tax charged is less than what should have been charged

Both must be declared in GSTR-1 for the relevant tax period.

## Penalties and Offences

### Late Filing Penalty:
- ₹50 per day (₹25 CGST + ₹25 SGST) for returns with tax liability
- ₹20 per day (₹10 CGST + ₹10 SGST) for NIL returns
- Maximum: ₹10,000 per return

### Fraud and Evasion:
- Short payment: 10% of tax or ₹10,000 (whichever is higher)
- Fraud: 100% penalty of tax amount

### Fake Invoice Fraud:
Issuing invoices without actual supply of goods/services to claim fraudulent ITC is a criminal offence under Section 132 of CGST Act, punishable with imprisonment up to 5 years.

## Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM)
Under RCM, the liability to pay GST shifts from the supplier to the recipient.
Applicable for:
- Goods Transport Agency (GTA) services
- Legal services by advocate
- Sponsorship services
- Services by Central/State Government (renting of immovable property)
- Import of services

## Composition Scheme
Small taxpayers (turnover ≤ ₹1.5 crore) can opt for composition scheme:
- Pay tax at flat rates (0.5%–6%)
- Cannot claim ITC
- Cannot supply inter-state
- Cannot issue tax invoices (only bill of supply)

## Place of Supply Rules

### For Goods:
- Intra-state: Where goods are delivered (CGST + SGST apply)
- Inter-state: Different state from supplier (IGST applies)

### For Services:
- General rule: Location of recipient
- Immovable property: Location of property
- Events: Location of event

## Invoice Matching for ITC Claims
The GST system matches invoices between GSTR-1 (seller) and GSTR-2A (buyer).
Mismatches result in:
- Blocking of ITC claims
- Notices from GST authorities
- Interest on wrongly claimed ITC at 24% per annum

## GST Audit
- Turnover > ₹2 crore: GST audit by CA/CMA mandatory (GSTR-9C)
- Department audit possible for any registered taxpayer
- Special audit may be ordered under Section 66
